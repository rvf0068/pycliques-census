* Why this is a priority ten-vertex original

The historical global P3 census left original McKay index *11708967* unclassified because its second completely pared clique graph already has 288 vertices, beyond the then-allowed intermediate cap of 110. This ten-vertex original is independent of the three unresolved nine-vertex originals (which are being investigated by Astra); it has a *stiff ten-vertex core*.

An exact graph reconstruction gives a useful structural identity:

\[
\boxed{G_{11708967}\cong O_1*S,}
\]

where \(O_1=\overline{K_2}\) is the two-pole suspension factor and \(S\) is the eight-vertex snub disphenoid (graph6 =GQyuzw=). The source file is taken from frozen ten-v28 with original graph6 =IQyuzz~~o=. The two suspension poles are derived from their graph neighborhoods, *not* assumed to have preassigned indices. After deleting the poles the remaining induced eight-vertex graph is independently isomorphic to the snub.

The snub disphenoid itself is already proved \(K\)-divergent by an exact retraction from \(K^4(S)\) onto \(O_{24}\). However, this result **does not by itself establish** the \(K\)-divergence of its suspension: the graph-join operation does not automatically preserve the clique behavior of each factor. In particular, Neumann-Lara's Connected Summand Theorem 4.6 applies to joins of *coaffine* factors; the snub is not coaffinable. This investigation makes no unproved transfer.

* A general double-cover identity for clique graphs of suspensions

For any nonempty graph \(H\), the maximal cliques of its suspension \(O_1*H\) are precisely the sets \(q\cup\{a\}\) and \(q\cup\{b\}\), where \(q\in K(H)\) and \(a,b\) are the two nonadjacent suspension poles. The two layers of clique vertices corresponding to a fixed pole are each complete, and a vertex from one layer is adjacent to a vertex from the other if and only if their underlying cliques intersect.

Consequently, for the categorical graph product \(\times\),

\[
\boxed{\overline{K(O_1*H)}\cong K_2\times\overline{K(H)}.}
\]

*Proof.* Identify the vertices of \(K(O_1*H)\) with pairs \((q,a)\) and \((q,b)\), \(q\in K(H)\). A missing edge occurs exactly when the chosen poles differ and the underlying maximal cliques \(q,r\) are disjoint. These are precisely the edges of \(K_2\times\overline{K(H)}\). \(\square\)

For the snub, \(K(S)\) has 12 vertices and 46 edges, so its complement has 12 vertices and 20 edges. The double-cover formula predicts 24 vertices and 40 *complementary* edges, hence \(\binom{24}{2}-40=236\) ordinary edges in \(K(O_1*S)\). The exact independent P1 reconstruction agrees with this, and the verifier also checks a complete graph isomorphism with the double cover.
