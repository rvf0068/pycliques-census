* Certified census update

We use the convention that a /complete/ is a nonempty set of pairwise adjacent vertices, and that a /clique/ is an inclusion-maximal complete. For a graph \(G\), \(K(G)\) is its maximal-clique intersection graph. Let \(P_0(G)\) be its complete closed-neighborhood dominated-vertex paring, and put \(P_{j+1}(G)=P(K(P_j(G)))\). Every \(P_j(G)\) has the same clique behavior as the corresponding unpared iterate, and the notation \(O_d=\overline{dK_2}\) denotes the complement of \(d\) disjoint edges.

The previous certified snapshot ten-v25 had \(1052\) original indeterminate ten-vertex graphs, including \(430\) with nine-vertex pared cores and \(622\) with ten-vertex pared cores. The complete certificates below prove exactly six more original ten-vertex graphs \(K\)-divergent and leave \(1046\) originals indeterminate, divided as \(430+616\). The three indeterminate original nine-vertex graphs remain unchanged.

The archived proof from merged census PR #100 records five complete ordinary graph retractions \(r:P_3(G)\to O_d\), each with a complete map on all source vertices, a disjoint list of antipodal target pairs, and raw-map checksums. Its independent replay checks that every antipodal pair is a nonedge, all cross-pair edges are present, the retraction fixes the target pointwise, and every edge in the full source graph is sent to an edge or a single vertex. The sixth original, McKay 11651749, has a previously archived complete \(119\)-vertex bijection \(P_3(G_{11651749})\cong P_3(G_{11611429})\), checked against every edge and nonedge. Transporting the known \(O_{15}\) retraction along this isomorphism proves its divergence.

An ordinary retraction \(X\to O_d\) preserves the target as a retract under clique iteration. Since \(K(O_d)\cong O_{2^{d-1}}\) and \(d\ge3\), these targets are \(K\)-divergent. The complete paring theorem therefore proves each original graph in the table \(K\)-divergent. The conclusion relies on positive exact witnesses and not on bounded-search timeouts or negative heuristics.

print("Pared third-iterate size:", len(sequence[3]), sequence[3].number_of_edges())
print("Exact abstract third-iterate agreement:", nx.is_isomorphic(sequence[3], independent_p3))

* Interpretation and outstanding work

The previous ten-v25 snapshot, the PR #100 immutable retraction archive, the global \(P_3\)-class isomorphism certificates, and the nine-v9 snapshot remain byte-for-byte unchanged. The v26 verifier compares every retained six-column record to v25, requires precisely the six proved deletions, rejects changed graph6 strings or any extra deletion, and confirms that the active ten-vertex root matches the frozen new v26 snapshot.

The five still-oversized third-iterate representatives are McKay 11505012, 11595632, 11656793, 11709142, and 11713984. Bounded native searches previously reached =LIMIT_REACHED= for the existence of an octahedral retraction, so their \(K\)-behavior remains indeterminate. For further research, prefer the public reusable =pycliques= retraction and coaffine algorithms with larger, explicitly recorded budgets and structural obstructions before constructing huge fourth iterates.
