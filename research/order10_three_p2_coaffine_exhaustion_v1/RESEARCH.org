* Exact theorem and consequences

*Theorem (solver-certified).* For each of the four originals listed above, the second pared clique iterate \(P_2(G)\) has **no coaffine weak endomorphism**.

A coaffine weak endomorphism \(f:X\to X\) is a graph morphism such that \(f(v)\notin N_X[v]\) for every vertex \(v\). The theorem is certified by two already implemented public exact search backends (=python= and =native=), with both =label= and =fiber= value orderings. Each of the sixteen declared searches must return =EXHAUSTED= with an empty map and strictly fewer than the allowed 100000 search states. Every source graph is independently reconstructed and authenticated. A separate historical native source commit and its Python/C++ source Git blobs are pinned exactly as in PR #107's P3 pilot.

*Corollary.* None of these four graphs \(P_2(G)\) has an ordinary graph retraction onto *any induced coaffinable graph*.

*Proof.* Suppose \(r:X\to Y=X[S]\) is an ordinary retraction and \(\sigma:Y\to Y\) is a coaffination. The composite \(f=i\circ\sigma\circ r:X\to X\), with \(i\) the induced inclusion, is a graph morphism. For every \(v\in V(X)\), the vertices \(r(v)\) and \(\sigma(r(v))\) are distinct and nonadjacent in \(Y\). If \(v\) equaled or were adjacent to \(\sigma(r(v))\) in \(X\), then, because \(r\) fixes \(S\), their images under \(r\) would be equal or adjacent in \(Y\), a contradiction. Hence \(f\) would be a coaffine weak endomorphism of \(X\). This contradicts the theorem for \(X=P_2(G)\). \(\square\)

Thus **at stage P2**, no octahedral target, no complement of a coaffinable cycle, and no connected-coaffine-summand join can occur as an ordinary retract. This does *not* exclude these targets at P3 or a later stage.

* Positive-later-stage control: the phenomenon is real

For original McKay 11713984 the completely checked P3 morphism \(f\), archived in =research/order10_11713984_coaffine_retract_v1/certificate.json=, has an exact fourteenth-power ordinary retraction onto \(O_{14}*\overline{C_7}\). This target is expansive and the original is K-divergent. Yet the exact P2 verifier above proves that its second pared iterate admits *no* coaffine weak endomorphism whatsoever.

Therefore nonexistence of coaffine weak endomorphisms at a given iterate is not preserved by applying the pared clique operator. The result explains why the present P3 tests cannot be replaced by an apparently easier P2 search, despite the substantial order difference.
