* Complete first-iterate classification of two stiff originals

Let \(P(G)\) denote complete sequential ordinary dominated-vertex paring,
\(K(G)\) the maximal-clique intersection graph, and
\(P_0(G)=P(G)\), \(P_{j+1}(G)=P(K(P_j(G)))\).
A /clique/ is an inclusion-maximal nonempty complete.

Write \(O_d=\overline{dK_2}\), and \(*\) for graph join. Two original
connected ten-vertex graphs from the frozen ten-v28 census were
previously unclassified by a third-iterate reconstruction cap:
McKay 11715667 and McKay 11716441. Both are *stiff* at the original
order-ten level. Neither needs the large third iterate.

*Theorem.* The original connected ten-vertex graphs with McKay indices
\(11715667\) and \(11716441\) are \(K\)-divergent.

*Proof.* For each source \(G\), the exact independent maximal-clique
enumerators and complete ordinary dominated-vertex paring yield
\(P_1(G)\) with *11 vertices and 46 edges*. An explicit complete
graph6 SHA-256 and exact list of complementary connected components
are independently authenticated in
=research/order10_v28_p1_coaffine_join_v1/positive_retractions.json=.
For both first pared iterates,

\[
\overline{P_1(G)}\cong C_7\sqcup K_2\sqcup K_2,
\qquad
\boxed{P_1(G)\cong O_2*\overline{C_7}.}
\]

The complement of each \(K_2\) is a pair of nonadjacent vertices, with
coaffination interchanging the pair. The factor \(\overline{C_7}\)
is coaffine by the cyclic rotation of the underlying \(C_7\); this
factor is connected. Thus the displayed graph is a Zykov sum of three
nonempty coaffine factors. Neumann-Lara's Three Summands Theorem,
Theorem 3.6 of F. Larrión, V. Neumann-Lara and M. A. Pizaña,
"On expansive graphs", /European Journal of Combinatorics/ *30*
(2009), 372--379, DOI =10.1016/j.ejc.2008.05.005=, implies that the
sum is expansive and hence \(K\)-divergent. (The connected summand
Theorem 4.6 would also suffice.) Because complete dominated paring
preserves \(K\)-behavior, \(K(G)\) and \(P_1(G)\) have the same
behavior, so each original \(G\) is \(K\)-divergent. \(\square\)

This proof makes no assumption concerning the unresolved nine-vertex
graphs Astra is investigating; it uses no search for a fourth iterate,
no conjectural homotopy implication, and no inference from a
resource-limit state. It is a complete computer-assisted theorem with
a small, independently checkable certificate.

* Broader first-iterate target-family screen

For possible generalizations the same independent source builder
checks *all 613 stiff ten-v28 originals*, not only these two. The
current quick positive theorem screen recognizes joins whose
complementary connected components are disjoint copies of \(K_2\)
and cycles \(C_n\). Each factor's coaffination is constructed
explicitly, and the corresponding join is certified expansive
when at least three coaffine factors occur (Theorem 3.6), or when
there are at least two and one connected (Theorem 4.6). A single
factor \(\overline{C_n}\) is divergent when \(n\ge8\) by Theorem
5.4 of the same paper.

The complete independently audited screen found precisely *two*
previously unresolved positives, the originals in the table.
Unrecognized targets are left indeterminate. A negative hit in this
restricted target family is *not* a convergence proof.
