* Problem and scope

Let \(G\) be original connected ten-vertex McKay graph 11713984, with original graph6 recovered from the historical ten-v25 archive. The exact third pared clique iterate \(X=P_3(G)\) has \(169\) vertices and \(12897\) edges, and its complement has \(1299\) edges and \(35\) triangles. The currently merged ten-v27 file still lists this graph as indeterminate; the new proof below certifies divergence and awaits a separately audited census promotion. The previous native pilot from census PR #100 had discovered a coaffine weak endomorphism of \(X\), but neither the general search nor the octahedral search gave a complete octahedral retraction certificate.

This note converts that old positive coaffine endomorphism into a permanent exact *ordinary retraction certificate*. The resulting target satisfies Neumann-Lara's Connected Summand Theorem, which *proves K-divergence* of the original graph. We also derive an exact homological consequence and a fourth-iterate size lower bound, without constructing \(P_4(G)\). This proof-only PR does not change the authoritative census lists.

* Exact coaffine endomorphism and idempotent power

The archived map \(f:V(X)\to V(X)\) has \(169\) entries, now preserved in =certificate.json= with exact Git-blob provenance, compressed byte encoding and SHA-256 checksum. The independent =verify.py= reconstructs \(X\) by the existing historical independent maximal-clique and paring implementations, checks its complete canonical third-stage graph6 SHA-256, and checks the two defining properties for every vertex and every edge: \(f(v)\notin N_X[v]\), and for every edge \(uv\in E(X)\), either \(f(u)=f(v)\) or \(f(u)f(v)\in E(X)\). These are the required conditions for a coaffine graph weak endomorphism.

The image \(S=f(V(X))\) has exactly \(35\) vertices, \(f(S)=S\), and the permutation \(f|_S\) has *fourteen 2-cycles and one 7-cycle*. Hence \(r=f^{14}\) fixes each element of \(S\), and its image is \(S\). Being a composition of graph morphisms, \(r:X\to X[S]\) is an ordinary graph retraction. The verifier explicitly checks its entire \(169\)-coordinate map and all \(12897\) source edges, rather than trusting this deduction without a computational witness.

** The exact 35-vertex target

Let \(Y=X[S]\) and \(H=\overline Y\). The complementary graph \(H\) has *fourteen components isomorphic to \(K_2\) and one component isomorphic to \(C_7\)*, with every component and vertex set authenticated by the permanent certificate. Therefore

\[
  \boxed{Y\cong O_{14}*\overline{C_7}.}
\]

Here \(O_{14}=\overline{14K_2}\) and the asterisk denotes graph join. This is a proper stiff-looking structural target but is *not an octahedron*: the extra seven vertices are important. We do not silently discard them or pretend to have an ordinary \(O_{14}\) retraction. This distinction is essential because a graph join does not generally retract onto an arbitrary join factor.

* Positive divergence theorem (Connected Summand Theorem 4.6)

We apply the theorem of F. Larrión, V. Neumann-Lara and M. A. Pizaña, /On expansive graphs/, European Journal of Combinatorics 30 (2009), 372--379, DOI =10.1016/j.ejc.2008.05.005=, Theorem 4.6 (Neumann-Lara's Connected Summand Theorem):

/If \(A\) and \(B\) are coaffine graphs and \(B\) is connected, then their Zykov sum \(A+B\) is expansive./

The Zykov sum is precisely our graph join. The hypotheses are checked here, not assumed from a graph signature.

- The first factor \(A=O_{14}\) has coaffination swapping the two vertices in every antipodal pair. The complete saved list of fourteen antipodal pairs is independently checked.
- The second factor \(B=\overline{C_7}\) is connected. Restrict the archived native coaffine map \(f\) to the seven vertices of this factor. This restriction is a bijective automorphism that maps every vertex to a nonneighbor (equivalently, one step around the complementary seven-cycle). We check every ordered pair of vertices of \(B\).
- The union of these factor automorphisms is checked to be a bijective coaffination of the 35-vertex join, preserving adjacency and nonadjacency on *every ordered vertex pair*. All edges between the two factors are independently checked.

*Theorem.* Original connected ten-vertex McKay graph 11713984 is \(K\)-divergent.

*Proof.* The complete 169-coordinate certified map \(f\) is a coaffine graph endomorphism of \(X=P_3(G)\). Its 14th power is an ordinary graph retraction \(r:X\to Y\), verified on every source edge, and \(Y\cong O_{14}*\overline{C_7}\). The two graph factors admit the explicitly verified coaffinations described above, and the second is connected. Neumann-Lara's Connected Summand Theorem 4.6 therefore makes \(Y\) expansive, hence \(K\)-divergent. By the ordinary retraction theorem, \(X\) is \(K\)-divergent. Finally, dominated-vertex paring preserves \(K\)-behavior, so divergence of \(P_3(G)\) implies divergence of \(G\). \(\square\)

The theorem applies to the *whole* 35-vertex retract; no unjustified deletion of the seven extra vertices or ordinary retraction to \(O_{14}\) is needed. This result improves the earlier homological conclusion to a definitive positive clique-behavior classification.

* General join formula for complement disjointness graphs

For any finite graph \(Z\), let \(D(Z)=\overline{K(Z)}\), the graph on its inclusion-maximal cliques whose edges represent disjointness. The following general statement is useful independently of the present example.

*Lemma.* For every \(m\ge1\) and finite graph \(Z\), the complement of the clique graph of the join \(O_m*Z\) is the disjoint union of \(2^{m-1}\) copies of the categorical graph product \(K_2\times D(Z)\), where graph-product adjacency requires an edge in each coordinate.

*Proof.* Every clique of \(O_m*Z\) consists of a choice of one endpoint of each of the \(m\) antipodal pairs and one clique of \(Z\). Two such cliques are disjoint if and only if their binary choices are opposite in all \(m\) coordinates and their \(Z\)-cliques are disjoint. The binary strings form a perfect matching of \(2^{m-1}\) disjoint edges under the antipodal relation, and the disjointness condition in the \(Z\) coordinate is precisely \(D(Z)\). This proves the asserted categorical product formula. \(\square\)

In particular, if \(D(Z)\cong C_{2s+1}\) is an odd cycle, its bipartite double cover \(K_2\times C_{2s+1}\) is a single cycle \(C_{4s+2}\). Thus \(\overline{K(O_m*Z)}\cong\bigsqcup_{2^{m-1}}C_{4s+2}\). This lemma also applies to other target graphs with explicitly known clique-disjointness graphs and may be useful for future research on divergent suspensions without adding a new graph-specific algorithm.

* Explicit first clique graph of the target

A clique of \(Y=O_{14}*\overline{C_7}\) consists of a choice of one endpoint from each of the fourteen independent antipodal pairs, together with a clique of \(Z=\overline{C_7}\). The graph \(Z\) has seven cliques. The graph on these seven cliques in which two are adjacent if they are disjoint is precisely a \(C_7\); the verifier recomputes this complete seven-vertex disjointness graph.

Thus \(K(Y)\) has exactly \(2^{14}\cdot7=114688\) vertices. Two such cliques are disjoint if and only if their binary words are complementary *and* their \(Z\)-cliques are disjoint. Pairing binary words into \(2^{13}\) complementary pairs, the complement of \(K(Y)\) is a disjoint union of \(2^{13}=8192\) bipartite double covers of \(C_7\). Since \(C_7\) is odd, its bipartite double cover is \(C_{14}\). We obtain the exact graph isomorphism

\[
  \boxed{\overline{K(Y)}\cong \bigsqcup_{8192} C_{14},
  \qquad K(Y)\cong\bigast_{8192}\overline{C_{14}}.}
\]

No \(114688\)-vertex graph is constructed; this follows from the complete canonical small graph certificate and the standard maximal-clique formula for joins.

* Topological consequence at the fourth pared iterate

For a graph \(T\) we write \(\Delta(T)\) for its clique complex and \(\operatorname{Ind}(H)=\Delta(\overline H)\) for the independence complex. Kozlov's cycle formula gives \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_7)\simeq S^1\) and \(\operatorname{Ind}(C_{14})\simeq S^4\); see D. N. Kozlov, "Complexes of Directed Trees", Journal of Combinatorial Theory A 88 (1999), 112–122, and the exposition in /Combinatorial Algebraic Topology/, Theorem on independence complexes of paths and cycles.

It follows that \(\Delta(Y)\simeq S^{13}*S^1\simeq S^{15}\), and

\[
 \Delta(K(Y))\cong \operatorname{Ind}\bigl(\bigsqcup_{8192}C_{14}\bigr)
 =\bigast_{8192}\operatorname{Ind}(C_{14})
 \simeq\bigast_{8192} S^4
 \simeq S^{5\cdot8192-1}=S^{40959}.
\]

The ordinary retraction \(X\to Y\) yields an ordinary clique-graph retraction \(K(X)\to K(Y)\), which splits the induced map on integral homology. As complete dominated-vertex paring is a clique-complex homotopy equivalence, we deduce the *unconditional* certified lower bounds

\[
  \boxed{\mathbb Z\text{ is a direct summand of }
  \widetilde H_{40959}(\Delta(P_4(G));\mathbb Z),\qquad
  |V(P_4(G))|\ge81920.}
\]

The order bound follows from the sharp elementary flag-complex vertex bound: if \(\widetilde H_k(\Delta(T);\mathbb F)\ne0\), then \(|V(T)|\ge2k+2\). A proof of that lemma was recorded in the previously merged =research/order10_five_p3_native_v1/RESEARCH.org=. This is a positive consequence of a complete graph retraction, not a resource-limit estimate or an asserted classification.
